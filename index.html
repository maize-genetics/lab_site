Reading the genome to design the crops we need.

We use the natural diversity of plant genomes to find the individual DNA changes behind complex traits — and increasingly, we teach machines to read that diversity, building foundation models that turn raw sequence into biological understanding for maize and thousands of other species.

01 — WHAT WE DO

From natural variation to designed crops.

Four connected threads run from the diversity in wild and cultivated genomes to the sustainable crops that agriculture will need under a hotter, more variable climate.

[ image: maize diversity panel / kernels ]
/01

Diversity & genome evolution

Any two maize varieties differ more than humans do from chimpanzees. We map that diversity with GBS, HapMaps, and pangenomes to understand how genomes evolve and where useful variation hides.

NAMpangenomesdomestication
[ image: DNA language model / embeddings ]
/02

AI foundation models for genomes

PlantCAD2 and GeneCAD read DNA the way language models read text — capturing evolutionary conservation and predicting gene structure directly from sequence across the flowering-plant tree of life.

PlantCAD2GeneCADdeep learning
[ image: cold field / N-efficient maize ]
/03

Nitrogen 2.0 & sustainable design

Most nitrogen applied to maize is lost to the environment. We're designing nitrogen-efficient, cold-tolerant maize that extends the productive season and cuts fertilizer loss, emissions, and cost.

CERCAnitrogen useclimate
[ image: field phenotyping / breeding ]
/04

Breeding & open bioinformatics

Ideas become software the community can use. The Practical Haplotype Graph, TASSEL, and imputation tools bring high-tech breeding within reach of programs from Cornell to sub-Saharan Africa.

PHGimputationBreeding Insight
